# What one day of large Ethereum transactions is made of

4 September 2026 14:00 UTC to 5 September 2026 14:00 UTC, Ethereum mainnet only. Source: every transaction in which one address changed one asset position by at least $10,000 (plus flash-loan and gross-volume supplements), typed by the rules in `scripts/type_registry.py`, investigated by `scripts/tx_types.py`, and annotated in `qual_notes.md`. Prices are hourly Chainlink marks for ETH and BTC and window-end marks for the rest.

Dear reader,

The day had 1,793,396 transactions; 67,572 of them moved $10,000 or more for somebody, $26.0 billion in all, 333 of them above $10 million. The question this iteration asked was not which of them were largest but what kinds of thing they were, and whether a machine could tell the kinds apart without being told any names.

**The loop, in numbers.** Codex had grown a registry of 58 transaction types on a five-hour window earlier the same day. Replayed on the full day it typed 90.5% of the large transactions and 93.0% of their dollars, leaving 6,455 transactions and $1.8 billion unexplained. Two rounds of reading the unexplained residue added 22 mechanical types and took coverage to 99.1% and 99.8%. A fourth round re-read one random occurrence of every known type and found three rules that claimed too much: one accepted any four-argument `Withdraw` event as an Aave fork, one called a vault deposit a trade because the receipt token had no price, one filed aggregator swaps as bilateral exchanges. After the corrections the 90-type registry leaves 81 transactions and $24.6 million untyped. Coverage is rule matching, not proof of intent; the report says so on every page.

**Exchanges first.** The single largest identifiable mechanism is exchange plumbing, found by behaviour alone: wallets that send to many destinations and receive from many sources, deposit addresses that forward everything they receive within the day. Deposits into such wallets totalled [$3.84 billion in 9,792 transactions](report.md), withdrawals $1.59 billion in 7,139, a net inflow of $2.25 billion; sweeps of deposit addresses added $1.06 billion and transfers between exchange-like wallets $750 million. The busiest sink was not a wallet but a 6,889-byte contract, [0xa9d1…3e43](https://etherscan.io/address/0xa9d1e08c7793af67e9d92fe308d5697fb81d3e43), which took $964 million in 852 deposits and paid 729 batches of withdrawals. Deposits peaked in the first hours of the window (over 800 an hour at 14:00 to 17:00 UTC) and fell to about 285 an hour overnight.

**The layer the pilot could not see.** Two minutes of data showed exchange flow and flash loans. A day showed the custody and settlement machinery underneath: a system of four vault contracts sharing one ABI (sweep a client's approved balance, pay out under a signed instruction, hand tokens to the calling hot wallet) that moved $248 million, including a [$27.9 million transfer](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x657573aa5f60a4f9457152fccecbe1d3e58a11972127fec35f22acd50ad095df) between two of its own vaults; a stack of accounts moving USDC in [$11 million](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x2f3c690b65baaebdbaf819f137193e3a17795f0fdb7c2f5f1b54f42c3786d7e6) and $5 million pieces by `execute()` calls, $366 million in 412 transactions, which turned out on inspection to be EIP-7702-delegated EOAs driven by operator wallets that pay the gas; payment rails that record a [32-byte reference per transfer](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x5cade5c4bf71de94d05c4baf4a9213270f32208ddc23e7c3ef7690cf09fa963c); routers that [split a fee](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x21be55c51f1f149976b220fe9605265c9e25bda6b22d84faacda6f2e08e2577c) of 74 basis points at the median; 1,431 per-customer forwarder contracts swept into hubs; and a cross-chain swap service whose calldata carries orders like `USDT(TRON)|<address>|0.05|bridgers|` next to [400 ETH](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x15697afb421d8d980bdabdd7883b94e62bc0ad8aeb9369a747b12b973f152e60). All of these are one-sided on Ethereum by design: the other half of the trade happens off-chain or on another chain, so a dollar ranking sees them as large payments to nobody. Typing them needed only the direction of each transfer relative to the called contract and whether the contract wrote a record.

**Money that is not money.** Flash loans borrowed $24.0 billion in 1,386 transactions (Morpho alone lent $22.1 billion), the largest single loan $399 million, and the borrowers' observed net in priced assets was $0.00 at the median and below zero in sum. Sandwich candidates numbered 136 legs, most of them by two bots in two USDC/WETH pools, with observed nets of a few hundred dollars over the day. Whatever these strategies earn, it is not visible in token transfers; it goes to the block builder as ETH or sits in tokens the registry cannot price.

**Supply and lending.** USDC was minted for $542 million and burned for $409 million; DAI $351 million and $257 million; the Sky peg-stability path converted $961 million, $490 million of it in seven transactions between USDC and sUSDS. Aave v3 saw $898 million of operations, Morpho Blue $178 million, and an Aave-v3 fork pool (SparkLend, by model memory) $273 million in 83 eight-figure moves that the inherited registry had missed because its Spark rule looked for an event that can never match. Bridges took $527 million out (LayerZero and OFT $371 million, CCTP $228 million) and brought $398 million in. Eighty-two tokens outside the registry were priced from their own swaps; the largest, USDG, turned out to be the counter-asset of a hot wallet that converted $55 million of USDC into it during the day.

**What the machine still cannot say.** The largest single class, [plain transfers between addresses with no behavioural tag](report.md), is 14,356 transactions and $7.5 billion, 88 of them above $10 million. The day profile tells us they are not exchanges, bots, or sweep addresses; it does not tell us what they are. That is the next question for a longer window: what the recipients do afterwards.

The full investigation, with every rule, every method's output and the evidence behind each new type, is in `report.md`; the notes the LLM wrote are in `qual_notes.md`; the four rounds are archived under `round_01` to `round_04`.
