# What happened on five chains in two minutes

5 September 2026, 09:31:23 to 09:33:23 UTC. Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon. Source: the 38 transactions that topped each chain's gas limit, priority fee, or total fee, and the qualitative notes in `qual_notes.md`.

Dear reader,

The two minutes were quiet by price and busy by machine. Ethereum's base fee sat near 0.07 gwei, so the tip was the whole price of a transaction, and the highest tips were paid by robots reacting to other robots. Here is what stood out.

**Ethereum: a small token got sniped, and a small buyer got arbitraged.** FOLD drew two of the three highest tips of the window. In block 25,910,247 a bot [backran a 1inch-routed FOLD trade](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x53c5a374bc2fcc7cd7a3b2403d8906cc69008a1bb833ff08067838a5b198c77e) from index 1 with an 18 gwei tip. Two blocks later a 1.235 ETH FOLD purchase at index 0 was followed by a [separate payment of 0.002975 ETH to the block builder](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x53a64ed68331561b9097c8243df85af6922f40fc4a1f749c4920a6f9fad4ee6a), whose address matches the block's miner, beaverbuild. Elsewhere a retail account [bought uPEG with 0.2 ETH](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x773374f66aea53100bc30a92c07ae53c5c6d1e6ee941685f6baf44b62c4ade41) through an aggregator and paid the largest fee on the chain; two flash-loan bots from one three-wallet fleet [rebalanced the uPEG pools](https://etherscan.io/tx/0xd620890c9ce91144a831b23deb3af3a7b4f7cd5499511caad2e8995ce39af50f) in the same block for well under 0.001 ETH each. The same fleet ran an [ELA arbitrage](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x2af8ff2c02d0479482c6314524f4447ead63c53a9b0c141c0ef6f7142816925c) worth about 0.0086 ETH gross. The chain's top tip, 36 gwei, went to [two empty self-transfers](https://etherscan.io/tx/0xbb85f0f3ec69e5445673408b41bb7927e15aedecc8151ba60da3509b8e204b8b) that look like cancellations and still landed at the back of their blocks.

**Base: bots that pay only when it matters.** One bot [bought ANYONE on Aerodrome and bridged it to Ethereum through LayerZero](https://basescan.org/tx/0xefb4cb5c399251f32102f7ccbe5ad3f3b6d71e594f3f8308eee8c62ede132c5b) in a single transaction, paying the chain's largest fee to sit first in its block. Another [bought 638 ZEN with 1.875 WETH](https://basescan.org/tx/0x3a3f6674bfdedd7402be76cfbd74957801fa88ef8df714b59933b847ef149ed4) at 255 times the base fee while its other 19 transactions paid almost nothing. The other fee outliers were configuration: a 1 gwei "fast" preset on a DAG purchase, and a [naira-stablecoin payroll](https://basescan.org/tx/0xa0667c13a15017a2e3e6bd2b465c38d484c2f8067c3fc8a2db0e17717f1077cf) paying 200 times the base fee because its backend still uses a legacy gas price.

**Arbitrum: no tip market, only work.** Every transaction paid exactly the base fee, so ranking by tip was meaningless. The big spenders were keepers: [GMX V2 order execution](https://arbiscan.io/tx/0x1b396c2cb0e8516853aa590cd0ddd9a906f6a7e186e7d5b908cdcbaca5af6a31) fed by Chainlink Data Streams reports, a [Chainlink OCR2 report](https://arbiscan.io/tx/0xb5ead73120b4e0aa7e097bdda851a60488c30ec03f517cb060a876adbeead2fb) submitted with a 100 million gas limit and 124 thousand used, and a [Superfluid distribution call](https://arbiscan.io/tx/0x50424772d6381d1536d79fae9aa7c21b52fa1d382990bba6ffa2dfa8c5cc9da9) that left its caller a tiny reward.

**Optimism: a machine burning gas for free, and a service that is 7 percent of the chain.** One contract was [called exactly once per block](https://optimistic.etherscan.io/tx/0xaa91d3d3b93e1b3a4de882c84ca97d7ee1b9e93e71c060db0885111a48461765), 60 times, by three wallets taking turns, each call consuming 7.4 million gas at a 1 wei tip; execution is nearly free here, so the whole call costs about 0.00000001 ETH. A [keeper paying out USDC withdrawals](https://optimistic.etherscan.io/tx/0x708132e124e438307fa0a9a5426aeb479a67f7fa8487823dd216679b70f03129) sent 107 transactions from one address, about 7 percent of the chain's user transactions in the window. An [exchange-style hot wallet](https://optimistic.etherscan.io/tx/0xa0c2421f1da1388be1f3160c9e57a3cf1a99b43ae97adbc0a30945e226798ef3) with nonce 8.2 million moved roughly 81,600 in USDC and USDT at a flat 0.1 gwei.

**Polygon: gas price is an auction, and people bid.** The window's biggest bid was 88,175 gwei per gas, 26.9 POL in all, to [propose first on a UMA YES_OR_NO_QUERY request](https://polygonscan.com/tx/0x2878e165fd31899d635a20993722463f59826ca539d1da651f6835e963e71289) with a 750 USDC.e bond, the shape of a Polymarket resolution. A relayer fired [six EIP-7702 delegation transactions in one block](https://polygonscan.com/tx/0xb03af3867ed43d01081c76feb2e80282b874ec13a207b7f51b5cdcb70e57ff62), four failing, with tips up to 10,940 gwei: a fight over control of some account. Polymarket's own relayers, 70 addresses strong, carried 120 gasless user actions, among them [one that burned 21 million gas with no visible effect](https://polygonscan.com/tx/0x1db46a66979eab538e967f35460dfcd6b4645fde58b2f61d68d54d5660de6277). An operator [unstaked LGNS for 155 positions through Multicall3](https://polygonscan.com/tx/0x91478029cafd0fe49804d92526cd4a56d7125dc02695782958006d9c18fc059f) and consolidated everything to one address, using the same helper contract that received 89 EIP-7702 delegations in the window: many wallets, one hand. Payout wallets kept buying block position 0 with [flat 5,000 gwei transfers](https://polygonscan.com/tx/0xcdff86da13662b15b57190e07bf9feb39a12117ba3224b39f53bff19b4c040d2).

**What to take from it.** Gas outliers come in three kinds. Most are configuration: round gas limits and tips that never vary. A few are races, and they cluster at index 0 on Polygon and index 1 on Ethereum, right behind the trade they react to. The rest are fleets, the same contract worked by rotating wallets, so counting senders overstates actors. Contract names given here without an onchain check are hypotheses, and two minutes is a glimpse, not a baseline.

The full investigation is in `report.md` in this directory.
